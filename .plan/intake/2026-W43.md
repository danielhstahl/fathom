**What was fixed, and why** (the three sentences the rule asks for, for each):

* *Six contract tests that were red when the beta gate was last assessed.* They are green: `python3 -m pytest
  tests/test_cli_contract.py::… tests/test_config_contract.py::… tests/test_risk_ranking_contract.py::…
  tests/test_segmentation_spike_contract.py::… tests/test_workspace_contract.py::test_lint_subcommand_is_clean_on_the_repo_root`
  → `6 passed in 10.00s` against the binary built from this tree. The cause was drift, not breakage — each
  of the six asserts a cross-artefact agreement (a document naming a degraded state, a config key the schema
  knows, a lint set clippy agrees with) and each had been left behind by the ticket that moved the thing it
  watches. Closed here because the beta gate had been quoting them as red; they were fixed by the tickets
  that moved the contracts, not by this one.
* *Five shipped commits with no trace in the changelog.* The release body is the changelog, so a ticket that
  lands without an entry is a ticket nobody hears about; the fix is `tools/gen_changelog.py` — an `audit`
  that reads every commit since the last cut and refuses the next cut until the notes account for each one. The
  five are now named in `CHANGELOG.md` (`e3.7`, `e7.1`, `e5.6`, `e7.8` and the `s3` storage correction),
  and `.plan/changelog-coverage.json` stays **empty** on purpose: an exemption is for an omission a
  human has accepted with a reason and an owner, and `C07` fails the next cut if a covered exemption
  is left in it.

**What the digest says out loud.** Zero external reports is not a quiet week, it is the state of the project:
the two things that would change it (a pushed tag, an announcement) are `m0.1` and `m0.2`, and both are
named above rather than absorbed into "no reports received".
